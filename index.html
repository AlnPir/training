L1

Hip thrust

The main glute exercise of the session. It loads the glutes hard while sparing the lower back.

Red = the main muscle working.

ProgressionAdd 2.5 kg (1.25 kg per side) once you hit 4 × 10. If the last set still had 4 or more reps left, add 5 kg.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Sit on the floor with your upper back against a bench. The edge sits just below your shoulder blades.
  • Roll the bar over your legs into your hip crease, on a thick pad. Full-size plates (bumper plates) make the bar easier to roll.
  • Feet flat, hip-width apart. At the top, your shins should be vertical, with knees straight above the ankles.

The rep

  1. Breathe in and brace.
  2. Drive through your heels until your body is flat from shoulders to knees.
  3. At the top, tuck your pelvis: pull the tailbone under you, the opposite of arching. Ribs down, chin tucked, eyes forward.
  4. Hold 1 s and squeeze your glutes hard.
  5. Lower until your glutes almost touch the floor.

Watch out

  • Go all the way down on every rep. Never cut the range short to lift more.
  • No arch at the top: the glutes push, not the lower back. Looking up causes an arch.
  • Knees travel far past the toes at the top: your feet are too close. Move them away.
  • You can't fully straighten your hips and feel it all in the hamstrings: your feet are too far. Bring them closer.
Getting started

Start with the empty bar on a thick pad, or one dumbbell held on the hips. If you feel it only in your thighs, adjust your feet first (see Watch out). Still no glutes after 2–3 sessions? Do glute bridges (the same movement lying flat on the floor) for 2–4 weeks, then come back. A hip-thrust machine, if the gym has one, is easier to set up.

L2

Bulgarian split squat

Glutes and thighs, one leg at a time, without loading the spine. Working each leg on its own also shows and evens out left/right differences.

Medium stride, torso slightly forward: glutes and thighs both work.

GlutesQuadsInner thighs
ProgressionGo up to the next dumbbells once you hit 3 × 10 on both legs. If you get fewer than 8 reps with them, go back and add reps up to 12 first.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Put your rear foot on a bench, laces down. Hold the dumbbells at your sides.
  • Take a medium-long stride: at the bottom, your front heel stays down and your front shin leans only slightly forward.

The rep

  1. Brace and lean your torso slightly forward, about 15–20°.
  2. Lower over 2–3 s until your back knee almost touches the floor.
  3. Drive up through your whole front foot, mostly the heel. The back leg only balances; it doesn't push.
  4. Do all reps on one leg, rest 90 s, then do the other leg.

Watch out

  • A longer stride with more lean shifts the work to the glutes; a shorter stride with an upright torso shifts it to the thighs. This medium setup trains both.
  • Keep your front knee in line with your toes. Don't let it cave in.
  • Start with your weaker leg, and do the same reps on both legs.
Getting started

Weeks 1–2: no weights, with one hand on a rack or wall for balance. Still wobbly? Keep the back foot on the floor instead of the bench. Then add 2 × 4 kg dumbbells.

Rotation exercise. It replaces the split squat for 8 weeks at a time, with the same sets, reps and rest.

GlutesQuads
ProgressionGo up to the next dumbbells once you hit 3 × 10 on both legs. If you get fewer than 8 reps with them, go back and add reps up to 12 first.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Use a box or bench about knee height: with your foot on it, your thigh is roughly parallel to the floor.
  • Hold the dumbbells at your sides.

The rep

  1. Put your whole foot on the box. Lean slightly forward and brace.
  2. Drive through the heel of the top leg until you stand tall. Keep the toes of the bottom leg pulled up so it doesn't push.
  3. Lower slowly over 2–3 s and touch the floor lightly.
  4. Do all reps on one leg, then switch.

Watch out

  • If you bounce off the bottom foot, the exercise stops working. Go lighter or use a lower box.
  • Keep your knee in line with your toes.
  • A higher box works the glutes more. If your hips shift or your back rounds, use a lower box.
Getting started

Start with body weight on a lower box, between mid-shin and knee height.

L3

Romanian deadlift

Glutes and hamstrings, worked in the stretch. It's the only exercise on the lower day that loads the lower back, so technique matters most.

The hips move back and the bar slides down the legs. The knee angle barely changes.

3 × 8–10
Rest
2:30
Effort
RIR 2
Tempo
Down 2–3 s · up 1–2 s
Muscles
GlutesHamstringsLower back (holds the position)
ProgressionBarbell: add 2.5 kg once you hit 3 × 10. Dumbbells: go up to the next pair; if you get fewer than 8 reps with them, go back and add reps up to 12 first.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Stand holding the bar (or two dumbbells) against your thighs, feet hip-width apart.
  • Use an overhand grip (palms facing you), shoulder-width apart.
  • Bend your knees slightly. This small angle stays the same for the whole set.
  • Breathe in, brace, and squeeze your lats (the big back muscles) to keep the bar close.

The rep

  1. Push your hips back. Your torso tips forward as a result.
  2. The bar (or the dumbbells) slides almost straight down, touching your thighs the whole way.
  3. Stop at the biggest hamstring stretch you can reach without rounding your lower back. That's usually around the knee or just below, but the stretch sets the depth, not a distance.
  4. Drive your hips forward to stand up. Breathe out at the hardest point.

Watch out

  • This isn't a deadlift from the floor. The bar never touches the floor, and the knees don't bend more at the bottom.
  • If the bar drifts away from your body, it's too heavy or your upper back isn't engaged.
  • Safety check: at the bottom, your hamstrings and back should be clearly under tension. If you suddenly feel a "release" as you go lower, your back has lost its neutral position. Stop the descent there.
  • If your grip gives out before your hamstrings, use straps.
Getting started

Learn the movement with a broomstick first. Hold it along your back so it touches the back of your head, your upper back and your tailbone. Push your hips back: if the stick leaves one of those 3 points, your back has rounded. Then start with 2 dumbbells of 6–8 kg or the empty bar.

Seated leg curl

The only exercise in the program that works the hamstrings by bending the knee. It keeps the back of the legs developing in balance with the glutes.

The knee sits in line with the machine's pivot point.

ProgressionAdd the smallest step the machine allows once you hit 3 × 12. If you then get fewer than 10 reps, stay at that weight until you're back to 12.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Line your knee up with the machine's pivot, the axis the arm turns around.
  • Set the lower pad just above your heels, and snug the thigh pad down on your thighs.
  • Lean your torso slightly forward and hold the handles.

The rep

  1. Curl the pad down and back as far as you can.
  2. Squeeze for 1 s.
  3. Return over 2–3 s to full extension.

Watch out

  • Keep your hips on the seat.
  • No swinging. If you need momentum, the weight is too heavy.
  • The seated version builds the hamstrings better than the lying one, because they're stretched at the hip.
Getting started

Start with a weight you could lift about 15 times. Ask someone at the gym to check the machine setup the first time.

Setup

  • Sit with the pads against the outside of your knees.
  • Hold the handles.

The rep

  1. Push your knees out against the pads.
  2. Pause briefly in the open position.
  3. Return slowly, under control.

Watch out

  • Don't cheat with your lower back, lean back or swing.
  • Upright or leaning forward both work; leaning forward brings in more of the big glute muscle. Pick one and keep it the same so your log stays comparable.
  • Don't let the weight stack slam down between reps.
Getting started

U1

Lat pulldown

Builds the width of the back and pulls the shoulders back. It's also the way to work toward a first pull-up.

Elbows drive down; the bar comes to the top of the chest.

Lats (back)BicepsRear shoulders
ProgressionAdd the smallest step the machine allows once you hit 3 × 12. If you then get fewer than 10 reps, stay at that weight until you're back to 12.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Set the thigh pad so it holds your legs down firmly.
  • Overhand grip, a little wider than your shoulders.
  • Sit tall and lean back slightly, about 10–15°.

The rep

  1. Start by pulling your shoulders down, away from your ears.
  2. Pull the bar to the top of your chest, driving your elbows down toward your back pockets.
  3. Pause briefly with your chest up.
  4. Let the bar go back up slowly until your arms are straight and you feel a stretch under your armpits.

Watch out

  • No swinging, and don't lean far back to move the weight.
  • Never pull the bar behind your neck.
  • Your shoulders don't shrug up at the bottom of the pull.
  • Get the full stretch at the top on every rep.
Getting started

Start with a weight you could lift about 15 times. If you want to learn pull-ups later, the assisted pull-up machine can replace this exercise.

ProgressionAdd the smallest step the machine allows once you hit 3 × 12. If you then get fewer than 10 reps, stay at that weight until you're back to 12.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Feet on the platform, knees slightly bent.
  • Use the close-grip handle (palms facing each other).
  • Sit tall with your back straight.

The rep

  1. Start with your arms straight and let your shoulders reach slightly forward. Your back stays straight.
  2. Pull the handle to your lower ribs, elbows close to your body, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
  3. Hold for 1 s.
  4. Return slowly to the stretch.

Watch out

  • Keep your torso still. Rocking back and forth means the weight is too heavy.
  • Don't shrug your shoulders up.
  • Don't round your lower back at the stretch.
Getting started

Start with a weight you could lift about 15 times. A chest-supported row machine is even easier to learn, if the gym has one.

Incline dumbbell press

Chest, front shoulders and triceps in one exercise. The incline adds a little more upper-chest work and is usually comfortable for the shoulders.

Bench at about 30°. The dumbbells go straight up over the shoulders.

ProgressionGo up to the next dumbbells once you hit 3 × 12. If you get fewer than 8 reps with them, go back and add reps up to 15 first.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Set the bench to about 30°, usually the second or third notch.
  • Sit with the dumbbells on your thighs. Lie back and use your knees to help bring them up.
  • Squeeze your shoulder blades back and down. Feet flat on the floor.

The rep

  1. Start with the dumbbells over your shoulders, arms straight.
  2. Lower them over 2 s to the sides of your chest. Your elbows sit about 45° from your body, not straight out to the sides.
  3. Keep your forearms vertical, wrists straight above your elbows.
  4. Press back up and breathe out.

Watch out

  • Don't let your elbows flare out to 90°.
  • No bouncing at the bottom.
  • A bench steeper than 45° turns it into a shoulder press.
Getting started

Start with 2 dumbbells of 4–6 kg. If a shoulder pinches, lower the bench angle and bring the elbows closer to the body.

Hanging leg raise

Direct work for the abs, since the lower-body day has none.

The pelvis curls up first, then the straight legs come up.

AbsHip flexors
ProgressionOnce you hit 3 × 12 with straight legs, slow the lowering to 3 s.
How to do itSetup, the rep, mistakes, getting started

Setup

  • Hang from the pull-up bar, overhand grip, hands shoulder-width apart.
  • Pull your shoulders slightly down instead of hanging limp. Elbows locked.

The rep

  1. First curl your pelvis up: bring your pubic bone toward your ribs.
  2. Then lift your straight legs to at least horizontal, breathing out on the way up.
  3. Lower under control and stop with your feet slightly in front of you, so you don't swing.

Watch out

  • No swinging, and no help from the arms.
  • If it only burns at the front of the hips, the hip flexors are doing the work. Curling the pelvis up is what brings in the abs.
Getting started

Step 1: bent-knee raises in the captain's chair, the station where you rest on your forearms. Step 2: bent-knee raises hanging from the bar. Step 3: straight legs. Move up a step once you can do 3 × 12.

Start here: the first 4 weeks

At the gym, open Lower day or Upper day above. Each exercise shows its sets and an animation; tap How to do it for the technique.

The first month is for learning the movements, not for lifting heavy.

  • Sets: do 2 sets per exercise (3 for the hip thrust). From week 5, use the full sets in the Lower day and Upper day tabs, but still keep about 3 reps in reserve. From week 6, use the effort targets too.
  • Effort: stop each set when you could still do about 3 more reps. Start weights are deliberately light: in weeks 1–2, if the last set still had 5 or more reps left, go up a step on the next set.
  • Form comes first: once a rep looks different from the first one, the set is over.
  • Film yourself once: in week 1 or 2, film one set of each exercise from the side. Compare it with the cues on each card.
  • Soreness: stiff, sore muscles for 1–3 days after the first sessions are normal. A sharp pain in a joint is not: stop that exercise.
  • Write down every set as kg × reps. That's how you know when to add weight.

Two basics

Effort: reps in reserve (RIR)

RIR is the number of clean reps you could still do when you stop the set. "RIR 2" means you stop with 2 reps left in the tank. The target is for the last set of each exercise. Earlier sets feel a bit easier, which is normal.

It's easy to stop too early. If the last reps still move fast, there were more than 3 left.

Bracing and breathing

For the hip thrust, split squat, step-up and RDL, the abs must be tight to protect the lower back.

Do
  • Breathe into your belly before lowering.
  • Tighten your abs all around your waist, as if you're about to take a punch. Belly and sides go hard.
  • Hold that tension through the whole rep, with your back neutral.
  • Breathe out through the hardest part of the way up. Keep breathing; don't hold your breath and strain.
Don't
  • Push your belly out on purpose.
  • Suck your belly in. Both kill the brace.

Machines and the upper-body day: keep the abs lightly tight and the ribs down, and breathe out on the effort.

Progression and recovery

Double progression

First add reps until every set reaches the top of the range. Then add weight and drop back to the bottom of the range. Change one thing at a time: reps or weight, never both.

  • Tired day? Do half the session instead of skipping it.
  • Food: eat at maintenance calories with about 1.6–2 g of protein per kg of body weight. For 60 kg, that's 95–120 g a day.

Why these exercises

Lower day

  • Hip thrust: very high glute activation, easy to load and progress, and gentle on the back. In a direct comparison, it grew the glutes about as much as squats.
  • Bulgarian split squat: glutes and thighs without a bar on the spine, and each leg works on its own.
  • RDL: glutes and hamstrings in the stretched position. Three sets and the safety check keep the back load in check.
  • Seated leg curl: trains the hamstrings at the knee, which the RDL can't do. In a direct comparison, the seated version grew the hamstrings more than the lying one.
  • Abduction: the upper and side glutes, with almost no load on the back or pelvic floor.

Upper day

  • Lat pulldown: back width and posture, and the path to a first pull-up.
  • Seated cable row: the middle of the back. With the pulldown, it gives two pulls for one push.
  • Incline dumbbell press: chest, shoulders and triceps in one exercise.
  • Lateral raise: the side of the shoulders, which the press works much less.
  • Hanging leg raise: the only direct ab work in the program.
